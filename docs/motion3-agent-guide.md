# Motion in CozyClay 3: how the agent authors it

The agent writes a **motion spec**. The deterministic compiler bakes every frame on the real rig (X Bot or Y Bot), fixes small mechanical errors itself, and QA measures the result. Every QA issue group says what caused it and what to change. The agent edits the spec and compiles again until QA reports nothing. A 730-frame scene compiles in about 150 ms and QA takes about 80 ms, so iterate freely, normally inside one `script` call.

`motion.compile` thins redundant free-span keys by default. The receipt reports `output.thinned {removed, kept, before, after}`. Use `thin: false` to keep every authored key when the spec is generated per frame.

## Loop

1. Read the scene with `inspect_studio`: object ids, positions (base pivot = bottom centre), sizes, the character (`model`, `x/y/z`, `rot`).
2. Plan the beats with frame ranges at 24 fps. **Build them from skills first** (`spec.use`), then add your own keys for what no skill covers.
3. `run_action motion.compile { characterId, spec, install: false }`. Read `output.qa.groups`: each group has a `cause` and a `hint`.
4. Apply the hint of the largest group. When a value is a guess (a turn radius, a heading, a height), write it as `{ range: [lo, hi] }` and let the compiler search it. Compile again. Repeat until `qa.ok`.
5. `motion.compile` with `install: true`. Then do one `verify_result` contact sheet as a visual check: `frames: "beats"` with `views: ["side", "front"]` in a scene with walls. Read `hidden` and `seeThrough` in `visualRefs[0]`.
   To show a take from one still camera: `frame_shot { subjectIds, framing: { intent }, over: "shot" }` frames every pose of the shot, goes farther back on a longer lens or to the mirrored view, and turns away only as far as walls need. Read `checks.held`: `chosenDeg` can differ from `askedDeg`. To show the user something in the viewport: `run_action view.frame { position, lookAt }`; the shot camera does not change.
6. Report the QA numbers, `output.fixed` (what the compiler changed for you), what was declared in `checks`, and anything that was not checked.

## Transition between two poses (free spans): MotionBricks fill (`motion.fill`, opt-in)

The fill is a **transition between two poses (free spans)**. MotionBricks, a local motion model the dev server runs, generates the motion between neighbouring authored key poses. It is off by default: `motion.compile` fills only when the spec says `fill: true`, and `motion.fill { characterId, spec }` fills on request. Use it for a free pose-to-pose move no skill covers (sit down, stand up, "A pose to B pose"). Keep contacts and plants on your own keys.

Author 2-4 whole-body key poses. Each pose keys root, `yaw/pitch/roll`, `spine_*`, `head_*`, `foot_l/r`, `knee_l/r`, `fyaw`/`fpitch`, `hand_l/r` with `elbow_l/r`, and `armw_l: 1, armw_r: 1` on the first key. Each gap between neighbouring authored keys (at most 46 frames per model call) becomes keys marked `src: "fill"`. The filled spec then compiles, runs QA and installs like `motion.compile`, and QA names fill keys `keys[n]` like your own. To change it, edit a key (an edited fill key drops its `src`) and send `motion.fill { base, at: <its frame> }`: only the two gaps beside it are filled again. Fingers, wrist twist and props are not filled. `motion.describe { topic: "fill" }` has the details.

In the Studio, Pose mode has the same workflow on the Sequencer's key bar: **Key** (K) keys the whole pose and the character's spot at the playhead, Shift+K removes it, **Frames** sets the timeline length, and **Fill transition (free spans)** runs `motion.fill` over the keyed frames.

### Keys in a scene with objects (stairs, a carried box)

Values from a take with no overlaps: climb, take a box from a table, turn and carry it down (X Bot, 0.3 m box, steps 0.3 m deep and 0.17 m high, 2026-11-01). Each rule is a mistake that cost a round.

- Space keys about 18 frames apart and move one foot per gap. A foot keyed at the same spot and heading at both ends of a gap stays planted through it; two feet moving together read as a shuffle. Check the keys alone first (`motion.compile` without `fill`): a key frame with QA on it is the author's, not the fill's.
- Feet on a step: ankle `y` = the tread's top + the rig's ankle height (0.087 on the X Bot). Put the ankle 0.20 m short of the edge the toes point at, going up and going down: a toe in the next riser is a `COLLIDE`.
- Turning: turn on the spot in 45-degree keys, one foot stepping per key, the feet on a 0.1 m circle about the root and the hands ahead of the body at each key. A named-pose key that sets `yaw` turns the feet with it unless it keys `fyaw_l/r`, so a key that leaves a foot behind keys that foot's `fyaw`.
- Taking a box in both hands: `objects.box: { prop, grab: <f>, mass: 0.3 }` on one hand. Three keys, 16 frames apart: hands raised ahead of the table, `x = ±0.30`; then beside the box at its `z`, `x = ±(half width + 0.18)`; then the grab key at `x = ±(half width + 0.04)`, `y` = the box's middle + 0.025. The next key lifts straight up about 0.17 m before anything moves back. The other hand stays on the box after the grab by itself (`fix.grasp`).
- Weight lowers a held box 6 mm per kg, so a box lifted off a table first sinks into it: keep `mass` under 1 for a light box.
- Carrying: hands 0.42 m ahead of the root and 0.14 m above it, elbows `[±0.4, -0.85, -0.35]`. Lower, the box hits the thighs on a step down. Standing up at the bottom, hold the hands 0.6 m ahead with the elbows out (`[±0.6, -0.72, -0.35]`) so the box clears the torso and the forearms.
- Walking and turning with a box in both hands: key `objects.box.keys` through the carry with no `prop` (the keys move it), against the chest while walking (its back face about 2 cm off the body; a key every 6 frames and every 2 through a turn on the spot: sparse keys cut the circle and put the box in the hips), off the chest only to raise it onto a shelf. Put each hand on it with `reachGrip` (`hold` to the release) on its own object anchor `{ kind: "object", object: <box>, grasp: "support" }`, with no `local` and no `fingers`: the compiler puts the palm flat and low on that hand's side face and closes the fingers round the bottom edge, under the box, from the box's size and the rig's hand. While the box rests on a table, a shelf or the floor the hand presses its side higher, out from under it; it slides down under the box as the bottom clears what it stood on, rolls round the bottom edge when the box goes up past the elbows, and draws the fingers out just before the box lands (setting it on the floor, it slides up the faces until the fingertips clear the floor); at the let-go it takes the hands off the faces and off what the box stands on before the arms drop, so no return keys are needed. QA says `PALM_OFF` when a palm is off its face and `TIPS_OFF` when fewer than three fingertips of a hand are under the bottom while the box is 0.1 m or more clear of what is under it (6 frames either side; nearer, the fingers are out on purpose); a `local` on the anchor pins the palm where it says, and `TIPS_OFF`'s patch removes it. Set by hand (`grasp: "palm"` and a `local` on the face), the agents' A/B T2 runs (walk, take a 0.3 m box from a table, turn, set it on a 1.2 m shelf) put the palm 0.10-0.15 m up the face, where the fingers never reach under the box.
- A `w_<anchor>_<side>` key with no fit puts only the wrist on an object anchor: the palm does not turn to the face (its `grasp` turns nothing) and the hand may not touch the box, so QA counts it in the box. When the anchor has a `grasp`, or when moving its point would put the wrist inside the object, the QA hint names `reachGrip` instead. The automatic other hand (`fix.grasp`, above) holds a hand that `hand_<side>` keys put beside the box at the grab; it leaves a hand on an anchor alone.

### Native mode (opt-in, dev server)

`COZYCLAY_MB_MODE=native` on the dev server (`tools/dev-full.mjs`) switches `motion.fill` and `fill: true` to `src/motion3/bricks/fill-native.js`, which drives the model the way the upstream runtime does: 4 target poses (with velocity) at a key the motion passes through and 1 (a stop) at the last key, engine-style endpoint correction, and dense output keys. `COZYCLAY_MB_TIMING` picks the timing: `exact` (default, authored frames kept, the model's own length comes back as a `timing` warning), `free`, `retime` (each gap takes the model's length and later keys move) or `playground` (one chain, middle keys are guides that come back in `output.fill.guides`, the result installs without repair or thinning). `COZYCLAY_MB_GATE=0` also generates the gaps the default fill leaves to the compiler. Without these variables nothing changes.

Smart Primitives on a scene object: `node tools/motionbricks/vault.mjs --block x,z,width,depth,height --objects objects.json [--doc jump_0_75m] [--seq OneHandedJump0]` writes a spec that installs as is (`motion.compile` with `repair: false, thin: false`). It places the official SOMA Smart Primitive targets on the block, runs the chain like the upstream runtime (target-pose IK pins included), and corrects block overlaps (QA COLLIDE) and joint pops (QA POP/FLIP) inside the chain so the next step continues from the corrected frames. `node tools/motionbricks/poses.mjs [out.json]` exports the 22 target poses as pose-library entries. Both fetch NVIDIA's sample clips at a pinned commit into `~/.cache/cozyclay/motionbricks/`: they are under the NVIDIA Sample Data Evaluation License (evaluation and testing only) and must not be committed or shipped.

### Model setup (dev server only)

- The fill runs on `onnxruntime-node`, which is not a dependency of the app or the MCP package. Install it once next to the server: `(cd mcp && npm install --no-save onnxruntime-node@1.23)`.
- The weights are not in this repository. On first use the dev server (`npm run dev`) downloads the MotionBricks SOMA ONNX model from the model's upstream repository and checks its SHA-256. You can also download the file yourself and put it in the cache directory.
- Cache: `~/.cache/cozyclay/motionbricks/` (or `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/cozyclay/motionbricks/`). `COZYCLAY_MOTIONBRICKS_MODEL=<path to the .onnx file>` uses a file somewhere else.
- Precision: fp32 by default (`mmm_v6_soma_fp32.onnx`, 758 MB, about 15 ms per call on CPU). `COZYCLAY_MB_PRECISION=fp16` uses `mmm_v6_soma_fp16.onnx` (380 MB, slower on CPU, up to 3.4 cm drift).
- A static build has no Node side: `motion.fill` answers `CAPABILITY_MISSING`, and `fill: true` compiles with the default interpolation and a note in `output.fill`.
- License: see `mcp/motionbricks/NOTICE.md`. The user fetches the weights under the model's own license, and no sample motion data is shipped.

## Skills (`spec.use`)

`use: [{ skill, args, f0 }]` expands, in order, into keys, anchors, props, fits, objects and checks before compile. Each skill starts from the pose the earlier keys leave at its `f0`. Your own `keys` come after the skill keys, so your key on the same frame wins; your own anchors, props and fits replace a skill's of the same name. `output.skills` lists where each skill ends.

| Skill | Args | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `standIdle` | `at: [x, z]`, `heading`, `until` | Neutral standing pose, arms down (FK). The start pose for a walk. |
| `walkTo` | `to: [x, z]` or `heading: h \| [h0, h1]`, `steps`, `step`, `turnDistance`, `halfWidth`, `lift`, `firstStep`, `period`, `vary = 1` | The walk generator from the pose at `f0`. With `to`, it turns toward the point and picks the step count. Ends `16 + steps * period + 18` frames later, feet together. The steps keep a natural rhythm: the first steps build up, the last one slows into the stop, one side is a little quicker, no two steps are alike, and turns take shorter steps; `period`, `step`, `lift` and `armSwing` are averages, and the walk keeps its frames and its end point. `vary: 0` makes every step exactly `period` frames and `step` m; `2` is twice as uneven. |
| `reachGrip` | `anchor`, `side`, `define`, `reach = 20`, `hold = 22`, `release = 12`, `elbow`, `fingers = "grip"` | The hand goes to the anchor with the fingers closing, holds, lets go. Adds the fingertip fit and a contact check. |
| `sitInSeat` | `car` or `seat` (+ `seatBack`, `wheel`), `offset = [0, 0.17, 0.02]`, `floor` | Seated at the wheel, hands forward. Adds the seat support and the seat allowances. |
| `exitCar` | `car` or `seat` + `door` + `handle`, `side = "l"`, `channel = "door"` | Reach the inner handle, open the door, swivel, legs out, stand up with the hands on the thighs, two steps away. 246 frames. Follows the car's position and yaw; `side: "r"` mirrors it. Tuned on the X Bot for a seat base at y 0.4 m. |
| `smokeCigarette` | `until` | Cigarette from the right pocket to the lips, lighter from the left pocket under the tip, light, exhale, look around, a second drag. Needs 265 frames. |

`car: "Car"` fills the ids `Car_Seat_Driver`, `Car_Seat_Driver_Back`, `Car_Door_L` (or `_R`), `Car_Door_Handle`, `Car_Steering_Wheel`. The whole car-exit scene is four lines:

```js
use: [
  { skill: "sitInSeat", f0: 0, args: { car: "Car", offset: [-0.06, 0.17, 0.023] } },
  { skill: "exitCar", f0: 0, args: { car: "Car" } },
  { skill: "walkTo", f0: 246, args: { heading: [125, 150], steps: 8, turnDistance: 0.4 } },
  { skill: "smokeCigarette", f0: 440 },
]
```

## Reading QA: cause and hint

Every group: `{ kind, subject, count, first, last, worst, worstFrame, limit, cause, hint }`.

- `cause` names the spec element most responsible at the worst frame: a key and channel (`keys[12] f200 hand_r`), the two keys around the frame (`root between keys[3] f84 and keys[7] f97`), an anchor and its fit (`anchor mouth (fits[1]) via keys[50] f498 w_mouth_r`), a generator step (`generators[0] walk step 3 f316 root`), or a skill (`use[1] exitCar f116 foot_l`).
- `hint` says what to change, with a direction and an amount in world metres or degrees, at a frame. Examples:
  - `root: move [0, +0.026, 0] m at f0 (out of Car_Seat_Driver)`. Add the vector to that key.
  - `retime: stretch f300-f312 by 1.4x (+5 frames, move the keys from f312 on later)` (fix.retime, on by default, already tries this).
  - `cig_vis: move the step from f454 to f460 (RightHand 0 m from rpocket there)`.
  - `elbow_r: key [+0.5, -1, +0.5] (body frame: +y up, +z forward) at f497`.
  - `walk path: root needs [+0.049, 0, +0.122] m at f315 (out of Car_Door_L); change that walk's heading/turnDistance/step (or give one a { range: [lo, hi] })`.
- `FLOAT` (off the ground, the hips not falling) fits the ballistic arc between the frames just before takeoff and just after landing (gravity fixed): `hips should be 31 cm higher at f28 (ballistic arc from f22 to f34)`, and its patch moves the root keys at 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4 of the run onto it (and keys the root at its own height at takeoff and landing when no root key sits within 2 frames). A run that needs more than 0.5 m gets numbers instead of a patch: an N-frame flight peaks g (N / fps)^2 / 8 above takeoff, and the hips' own apex h fits fps sqrt(8 h / g) frames (`hips held 1.43 m below a ballistic arc over 27 frames (f22-f49): either raise the apex to 1.55 m above takeoff, or shorten the air run to 8 frames for this apex (0.12 m)`). A run that reaches the end of the take gets the words only.
- `DROP` (supported, the hips accelerating down faster than 1.1 g for 2 frames or more, going down or up): a support only pushes, so a body on its feet falls at 1 g at most, and a rise stops at 1 g at most. The subject is `hips fall at 2.0 g with support`; the hint gives the drop and the time it needs even at 1 g, sqrt(2h/g): `the hips drop 0.35 m over 6 frames (f20-f26) at up to 2.00 g while a foot or the body is supported; a drop of h m takes sqrt(2h/g) = 0.27 s (7 frames) even at 1 g: key the root's low point at f27 or later, or brace earlier: put a hand on the ground or an object before f21 so the arms take the fall`. A rise that stops too fast reads `hips stop rising at 1.8 g with support`, and its hint gives the time v/g that stopping the rise needs at 1 g. A body turning over (the hips' up axis tilts more than 180 deg in one go: a cartwheel, a flip, a roll) is left out: its hips swing round what carries it. A turn about the vertical (a pirouette) is no turn over.
- `SPIN` (in a flight or a free air run the take cuts, the angular momentum about the centre of mass drifting): nothing turns a body in the air, so its angular momentum stays what it left the ground with, and a body turns faster tucked and slower open. Read on the frames between takeoff and landing, along the flight's mean momentum axis only: `SPIN` judges how much of the momentum lies along that axis, not where the momentum points, so a momentum whose direction swings in the air (a keyed twist that tilts it 46 deg off its mean axis) reads no `SPIN` while its size along the axis holds. A direction rule does not separate the cases: the 360 deg spin jump with its forearms folded holds 47 deg off its axis after `fix.spin`. The limit is 20% of the larger of the mean and I x 4 pi rad/s, I the flight's mean inertia about that axis (segment masses and de Leva 1996 radii of gyration, a 70 kg body). As turn rates: a frame may turn 20% faster or slower than its momentum allows, and at least 2.5 rad/s off it when the body turns slower than two turns a second. A spin jump keyed at an even rate with its arms pulled in reads `SPIN`. `fix.spin` (on by default) re-times the root's turn so it holds, and never leaves a flight reading more than 0.02 over what holding the momentum along the turn's axis alone leaves (`fixed.spin.axis`); with it off, key the turn fastest where the limbs are pulled in. A jump with no turn (a vertical jump with an arm swing) has a momentum near 0, so its size drifts by more than its mean however it is keyed; neither fix changes it there (the arc moves the whole body, which changes the momentum about the centre of mass by under 1e-13 kg m^2/s on every frame). A flight whose keyed turn turns back by more than 2% of its total is left as keyed (`fixed.spin.left`), and so is a run the take cuts. A flight whose root turns less than 30 deg has no turn to re-time: a hop with the feet tucked 0.45 m and no turn reads `SPIN` from the legs' swing alone, and only a counter-turn of the root or a smaller tuck helps. The hint says which of these happened on the flight: re-timed, left (with the reason), cut by the take, `fix.spin` off, a run that does not fall (`FLOAT`), or a turn under 30 deg.
- On the push-off and impact frames of a flight `fix.ballistic` moved or `fix.spin` re-timed (the frame before takeoff, the first two air frames, and the three frames from landing), `POP` reads only a joint that read `POP` on that frame before the flight fixes, at the smaller of its two sizes: the arc meets the keyed ground frames at its own speed in one frame, and that contact is the fix's, not a pop. A pop keyed there stays (an arm snap the overlap layer lands on the impact: 48 cm/f^2, blocking), and so do the keyed pops of a vault's push-off at their keyed 22 cm/f^2. A pop the fixes added there reads `POP` when it is over 25 cm/f^2, where `POP` blocks: a one-frame impulse that large is a snap, not a contact. So a fix that would add one gives the flight its frames back and says so (`pops` in `fixed.ballistic.skipped` or `fixed.spin.left`): a hit at 8 m/s launched on the arc read the hips 27 cm/f^2 and the hands 30 in one frame, and keeps its keyed launch. A flight left as keyed reads `POP` as any take does.
- Hints are local: they come from the worst frame. Apply one, compile, read again.

## Auto-fixes (`spec.fix`)

The compiler fixes small mechanical errors itself and reports each fix in `output.fixed` (frames, worst amount). QA still measures the final frames, and the limits are never loosened.

| Fix | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `reach` | on | A wrist target up to 5 cm past the arm's reach goes onto it through a soft knee, so the arm never snaps straight. An ankle target up to 5 cm past the leg's reach lowers the hips just enough, eased over 6 frames. Farther misses stay for QA (`REACH`). |
| `slide` | on | A sole point under the floor lifts its ankle onto the floor (`fixed.ground`), so a squat with the heels up stands on the balls of the feet. A planted heel or ball that drifts less than 1 cm stays where it landed. A foot that turns (`fyaw`, `fpitch`) on its planted ball with the heel up, or on its planted heel, pivots about that point whatever the drift (`fixed.slide.pivots`): a batter's back foot turns on its ball. |
| `clear` | on | A swinging foot whose skin enters a scene body lifts over its top, or moves back out through the side it entered when the body is too tall to clear. A still foot sunk into a top comes back up onto it. The compile reports it as `fixed.clear`. |
| `penetration` | off; `0.02` or `true` | A hand or forearm inside a scene box by at most this much past its allowance moves out along the box normal, eased. FK arms (`armw` 0) are counted as `skipped`. |
| `person` | on | An upper arm, forearm or hand inside another character (QA `PERSON`, their installed take) moves its wrist target out by the depth plus 4 mm, eased, over 6 fixed passes. The escape keeps the side the hand came from, so a hand keyed deep into someone comes back out the front, not through their back. No depth limit. `checks.allow { part, object: "person:<id>", depth }` lets a meant touch go that deep. FK arms (`armw` 0) are counted as `skipped`. |
| `self` | on; `false` | Arms out of the character's own body, on the fitted skin QA's `BODY` measures. First the elbow turns about the shoulder-wrist line (planned over the whole take) and a hand a little inside moves its wrist target out along the skin, eased (`fixed.self`: `elbowFrames`, `maxTurn`, `handFrames`, `maxPush`). Then the repair: where an upper arm, forearm or hand is still in the torso, a shoulder, the hips, neck, head, a thigh or the other arm's upper arm or forearm past what QA allows (`BODY`: `checks.limits.skin`, 1.5 cm, or a declared `checks.allow`; `FACE` for a hand in the head, past `checks.limits.headAllow`: QA's `FACE` takes no `checks.allow`, so a hand meant at the face goes on an anchor or a fit), the deepest hit over the whole take goes first. On that frame an arm on its IK moves its wrist target out along the skin's normal (slid back toward the shoulder when the arm would lock straight, so the elbow bends) or turns its elbow; an FK arm (`armw` 0) moves by its angles: it is raised out from the body (its `arm_rest` less that angle, its `aswing` kept, so a swinging hand passes beside the thigh with the same phase and reach) or its elbow bends; an arm between FK and IK takes both kinds, each as far as it is on it. The move spreads over the 10 frames either side with an ease that reaches 0 at the window's edge; an FK move is first tried held over the whole run of frames with a hit around it (gaps of up to 20 frames joined), and of the moves that clear the frame a held one wins, the arm raised out before the elbow bent, then the smaller move of the elbow and wrist: arms hanging into the thighs through a swing or a walk take one angle, as if keyed to hang wider, so the hand passes beside the thigh without waving in and out (its path from the hips within 12% of its length). Frames on which the keys hold the arm still with its hand on the body (every channel but the other arm's as on the frame before, the wrist or middle knuckle within 4 cm of the torso, hips or a thigh, or of the other arm where that arm has no hit of its own: hands keyed on the hips, a hand held at the far hip against the hanging arm) count as one, so that hold takes one constant move and stays still. A move that adds a `POP` or a `FLIP` to the arm, makes the elbow's or the wrist's `POP` worse the way QA groups it (more pops, or one harder than that joint's hardest in the take), or deepens the window's deepest hit is not made, and one that adds a `BODY` or `FACE` subject QA did not report only when nothing else helps. No move takes the hands into each other (`SELF` `hands overlap`). A free IK hand the repair leaves more than 2 cm farther off a key you wrote (a key outside the skin, on its key frame) is a `KEY` problem, `hand_r off its key (fix.self)`, one group a hand, sized by the most the repair added and its hint giving the wrist off the key before and after (`QA`'s `REACH` reads against the target the repair pushed, so this is how a hand pulled off a cup it reached for is seen); the gate below weighs it with the rest of `QA`; with crossed arms the two take turns, the deeper first, and stack on the chest. Again until nothing is past the limit, 48 moves are tried for each run of frames with a hit, or the take's budget is spent (160 moves tried and 40000 frames solved over every search, however long the take, so a long take compiles in time near its length: crossed arms re-keyed every 30 frames over 960 frames 8.0 s against 3.3 s without the repair); a run of frames with the hand on the body whose 3 deepest frames take no move is not tried further, and where the first moves made none the wider set is not tried (a take the repair keeps nothing on compiles near what it does without the repair); then, on what is left, the same again with a wider set as well: the wrist over the skin (forward, up, out to the arm's side, as well as along the normal) by the depth times the lever from the shoulder to the hit, up to 0.3 m in all, and elbow turns of 120 and 150 deg. Pairs joined at a joint (the upper arm in the chest at the shoulder) never count. A hand the take puts somewhere keeps its place: on a body anchor, a fit, another anchor, a grasp, a `checks.contacts` entry, or keyed together with the other hand (both IK hands' targets within 12 cm on the frame and on the keys either side: a clasp or a clap, whose arms on each other are not repaired and `QA` reads as keyed) only the elbow turns, and a wrist push or an FK move spread toward it fades with that weight. Each span of frames the repair moved stands only where QA is no worse with it (the rule that keeps `retime`: no more problems, at as many no higher summed severity, no goal lost), no other kind of group rose but that `KEY` (a contact, a grasp, a support, a planted foot, a carried object, a collision with an object or a person) and it took 2 mm or more off its deepest hit or cleared a frame; else the span gets its frames back exactly (the spans are tried together, then in halves, so one bad span does not take the others back). Where every span goes back, the search runs once more with only moves that add no `BODY` or `FACE` subject and is gated the same way (not where a search ran out of moves) (crossed arms whose forearms would leave the other arm with a hand into the head or onto the other arm). A take with no arm past the limit comes out unchanged. `fixed.self` then adds `spans` (`at`, `depth` [before, after] m, `key` [before, after] m: the keyed hand the span moved most off its key), `depth` [before, after] (the deepest hit over the take), `iterations` and `left`, the frames still past the limit grouped by side and why, at most 7 entries a side (`at` first-last frame, `side`, `frames`, `why`: undone and why, the hand held where no elbow turn clears it, no move or no FK move within the limits and what turned down the moves that helped on the frame (a pop, the hands into each other, a frame around it deeper, the elbow lifted), the move cap, the take's budget); the `BODY` and `FACE` hints say which happened, or that `fix.self` is off. Keyed takes on the X Bot and Y Bot: a forearm across the belly 12.1 cm -> 0 (its hand 11.8 cm farther off its key, a `KEY`), a hand into the head 7.5 and 5.8 cm -> 0, a hand through the back of the head 11.8 cm (X) -> 0 and 10.1 cm (Y) -> 1.6 cm on one frame; FK arms hanging close (`arm_rest` 98) through a swing 10.7 and 6.1 cm -> 0 and through a walk with `armSwing` 45 10.2 and 7.7 cm -> 0, each hand's swing within 1% of its amplitude; both hands crossed onto the opposite shoulders 13.3 -> 8.6 cm (X Bot, QA 122 -> 50) and 10.1 -> 6.4 cm (Y Bot, QA 108 -> 89), read with the arms into each other as solved; a hand swept across the front into the far thigh 10.9 -> 2.9 cm (X) and 12.2 -> 0 (Y); a forearm across the belly to the far hip 11.5 -> 4.5 cm (X: the hanging left arm moves out of it first); a hand keyed across the body to the far side at waist height, out of reach, 12.1 cm -> 0 with its hand 18.9 cm farther off its key, read as that `KEY` (X). A straight arm on a held hand reaching across the belly is left (only the elbow may turn), and so is what no move within 0.3 m of the wrist clears (a hand target deep across the body, a far thigh's 2.9 cm on the X Bot): key that target out of the body. |
| `retime` | on; `false` | Key intervals around a speed or acceleration spike get longer, borrowed from the neighbouring key intervals. The frame count stays. Frame-range checks follow. A frame whose acceleration reads a velocity of that kept interval (the frame after its first key to the frame after its last) is not counted as too fast either, since only re-timing the fall could change it. That holds for the air runs of the final take too: when a later fix makes an air run in frames retime moved (a handstand whose feet leave the floor only after the leg fixes), the fixes start again from the keys with that run's key interval kept, unless holding it leaves more frames too fast than the first pass did (then the first pass's timing stands; `fixed.retime.air` says which). Holding a 3-frame run or a run a later fix made costs retime frames elsewhere, so the take with those holds stands only where QA is no worse than the take that holds only the falling runs of 4 frames or more (the rule that keeps retime at all: no more problems, at as many no higher summed severity, no goal lost); else that take stands and `fixed.retime.air` names the runs it re-timed and the two QA readings (`fixed.retime.held` names the runs kept as keyed when the hold stands). One exception: where the take without the holds has fewer flights of under 4 frames that `ballistic` moves, the holds stand whatever QA reads (a standing hop re-timed through its 3-frame flight reads a 2-frame air run the arc does not reach, 12 to 17 m/s^2 RMS off g; held, its flight rides the arc). The take without the holds is compiled only where retime reads different windows or times without them. The handstand as sent keeps its keyed air timing on both rigs. |
| `ballistic` | on; `false` | In each flight (3 or more frames with no foot, hand, support or skin on a surface, split where a hand is on something that can carry the body: a `world` anchor, a `person` anchor, an `object` anchor on an object the body does not carry, or a still world point; a hand on the body's own hip, thigh or carried object does not split it) the whole body (root, ankle and wrist targets) moves so the centre of mass follows the gravity parabola from the frame before takeoff to the frame after landing, at constant horizontal speed. A run of 4 or more frames whose hips do not fall is left to `FLOAT`, whose hint stages the fall in the keys (a sparse 1.2 m step-off over 18 frames: the hips miss 80% of g and the arc would need 0.75 m, past the limit anyway); a 3-frame flight, which `FLOAT` does not read, rides the arc however its hips were keyed (a run's 3-frame flights keyed level: the centre of mass 0.98 g off 1 g keyed, 0 after; the arc moves it g (3 / 24)^2 / 8, about 3.4 cm, at most), but only where its takeoff and landing are contacts: a sole point (heel, ball, toe tip) or a hand bone within 2 cm of a support top on the frame before and on the frame after, and none on its own frames. A flight's air reads a hand at 6 cm and leaves the toe tip out, so a stumble's hands 2 to 6 cm over the floor end it a frame late, and a toe on the floor can stay inside it; anchored there the arc moves frames the body is not in the air on, so such a flight keeps its frames (`fixed.ballistic.skipped` with `contact`: which frame touches or does not). A run the take's start or end cuts has no takeoff or landing to anchor on and is left as keyed; a run that needs more than 0.5 m, whose arc enters a scene body, the floor, another character or a reach miss the keyed flight stayed out of, or whose arc misses a goal the keyed flight met, keeps its frames and is reported (`fixed.ballistic.skipped`: `off`, the apex an N-frame flight has, g (N / fps)^2 / 8, the frames the keyed apex fits, `into` and `depth` when the arc entered something, `lost` with the goals it would miss, `contact` for a 3-frame flight without a real takeoff or landing): give it those frames or lower the apex. `fixed.ballistic` = `{ runs, frames, max (m), at }`. `KEY` reads the keys as they were before this fix and `spin` moved the body. |
| `spin` | on; `false` | In each flight whose keyed root turns 30 deg or more, the root's turn (yaw, pitch, roll) is re-timed along its own path so the angular momentum about the centre of mass stays as near one vector as a turn about the keyed axis can: faster while tucked, slower while open. A body that leans off its turn axis also carries momentum across the axis in proportion to its turn rate, and that counts too (a 360 deg spin jump leaning 16 deg, arms pulled in: |L| drifts 29.1% keyed, 2.7% re-timed; held along the axis alone it drifted 29.2%). `SPIN` reads the momentum along its mean axis, which holding the vector can drift more: where the vector re-time reads over 0.02 there, the re-time that holds the momentum along the axis alone is solved too, and the vector stands only where it reads at most 0.02 more than that and stays under `SPIN`'s limit wherever that does; else the axis re-time stands (`fixed.spin.axis` lists those flights: a 360 deg spin jump on the X Bot keyed at pitch -8 with its arms pulled in over the second half reads 0.176 held along the axis and 0.207, past the limit, held as a vector). The turn at takeoff and landing, and so the keyed total, stays; the limbs keep their own timing (the ankle and wrist targets and the poles turn with the root, and each foot keeps its aim in the body: `fyaw` and `fpitch` are read again in the new tilt). A run of 4 or more frames whose hips do not fall (`FLOAT`) is left for its hint; a re-time that enters what the flight stayed out of, misses a goal the keyed flight met, or leaves more drift than before past `SPIN`'s limit, gets its frames back (`fixed.spin.left`: `at`, `why`). `fixed.spin` = `{ runs, frames, max (deg the turn moved), drift: [before, after], at, axis?, left? }`. Runs after the other fixes, then `ballistic` once more (a turn about the hips moves the centre of mass). |

`fix: false` turns everything off.

## Open parameters (`{ range: [lo, hi] }`)

Any number may be `{ range: [lo, hi] }` (or `{ search: [lo, hi] }`, add `int: true` for whole numbers): generator params, key channel values or one vector component, anchor offsets, skill args. `motion.compile` searches the values: the midpoint first, then a grid (one parameter) or Halton points (several), then a shrinking pattern search around the best. It stops at the first value with QA 0, and it never uses more than `search.budget` compiles (default 32, max 64). Each trial compiles only the frames the parameters can change. `output.search = { values: { path: value }, evals, cost, window, ms }`; `cost` 0 means clean. The installed take keeps the chosen numbers.

Example: walking off past an open door. `turnDistance: { range: [0.2, 2.0] }` found 1.55 in 5 compiles (0.8 s) where 0.4 by hand hit the door on 93 frames.

## Conventions and signs

- World metres, +Y up, floor at y = 0.
- `yaw`: 0 faces +Z; **+ turns toward +X**, which is the character's left at yaw 0 (a left turn). `fyaw_l/r` and `spine_yaw`/`head_yaw` use the same sign: + looks or turns to the character's left.
- `pitch`, `spine_pitch`, `head_pitch`: **+ bends forward** (head + looks down).
- `roll`, `spine_roll`, `head_roll`: + leans to the character's right.
- `fpitch_l/r`: toe-down pitch. **+ lifts the heel** (toe-off, the toes stay flat), - lifts the toes. Range -60..90.
- Poles (`knee_l/r`, `elbow_l/r`) and `body` anchors are **body-frame**: turned by the root yaw only. +z forward, +x the character's left, +y up. Knees bend toward +z; the default elbow pole is `[0, -1, -0.3]` (down and back). Never interpolate between opposite poles: the pole passes through zero and the limb flips. Add an in-between key.
- `aswing` + swings the arm forward; `abend` + bends the elbow.
- `wtwist`/`wflex` are clamped to ±85 and ±70. A mirrored pose negates them.
- A hinge angle turns about its axis by the right-hand rule: about `[0, 1, 0]`, + turns +Z toward +X.
- `root` is the hips joint. Standing hips height is about 1.03 m on the X Bot and 0.99 m on the Y Bot (an adapted rig: its `report.measured.hipsHeightM`). **`foot_*` is the ankle**: it stands 0.087 m (X Bot) or 0.105 m (Y Bot) above the floor, not 0 (an adapted rig: `ankleHeightM`).
- Anchor names must not end in `_l` or `_r`: the side goes on the weight channel (`w_handle_l`).

The compiler validates the spec strictly and lists every problem with an exact message, one per line, e.g. `keys[3] (f32) sets unknown channel "fpitch_left"; did you mean "fpitch_l"?` or `keys[6] (f54) knee_l = [0,0,-1] points backward; knees bend toward +z.` Unknown channels, wrong vector arity, frames out of range, `fps` other than 24, unknown anchors, bones, objects, fields and skills are all refused.

## Rig templates

Not reachable from the agent tools yet: no command calls `adaptRig` or `registerRig`, and the Studio compiles in a worker that only knows the shipped X Bot and Y Bot. This section describes the module API for code that imports it.

A character whose skeleton is not the X Bot's or Y Bot's compiles once it is adapted: `adaptRig(raw, { template? })` (`src/motion3/rigs/adapt.js`) turns `{ units: "cm" | "m", bones: [{ name, parent, localPos, localQuat }] }` into a rig with the canonical Mixamo bones, and `registerRig(id, rig)` (`rigs/index.js`) makes `skeletonFor(id)` answer it. Every skill, pose and spec then compiles on it; write `root` and `foot_*` heights from its `report.measured` (`hipsHeightM`, `ankleHeightM`), not the X Bot's.

- Templates (`rigs/templates/<id>.json`): `mixamo`, `ue5` (Manny/Quinn, and the UE4 mannequin), `vrm` (VRM 1.0 humanoid names, and `J_Bip_` nodes), `unity-humanoid`, `rigify` (`DEF-`), `cc4` (`CC_Base_`), `daz-genesis8`, `daz-genesis9`. The template is detected from the bone names (`report.detected = { id, score }`); `opts.template` forces it.
- Rest pose: an A-pose's arms (and every arm, leg and foot's heading) go onto the X Bot's T-pose bind; a foot keeps its own pitch and its toe segment lies level, as the X Bot's does; the spine and head keep the source's posture. A whole-body turn about the vertical, of any angle, goes away (the facing is the mean of the hip and shoulder lines); a pelvis turned against the chest keeps half the twist each way. A Z-up source stands up; twist, roll, IK, metacarpal, face and root bones are skipped (`report.extra`). Bones the source lacks (fingertips, toe ends, a head top, a spine or neck joint, whole fingers) are made up at the X Bot's proportions (`report.missing`).
- The floor is the source's y 0 when its ankles stand 2 cm to 15% of the height above it, else the ankle is put at 0.039 of the stature (`report.grounded: "estimated"`). The source's horizontal placement is kept. Mixamo names match with or without the namespace (`mixamorig:Hips`, three.js's `mixamorigHips`, `mixamorig_Hips`).
- The sole is the toe joints' height (the tip 0.6 cm less) unless `raw.landmarks.sole` gives it, so the X Bot through the adapter has `sole.tip` -0.6 cm where its own rig says -0.7 (a take differs by about 1e-6 m).
- Measured from the rig: hips and ankle height, the sole (ball and toe-tip height, the heel point scaled by the foot), the stance width (`standIdle`, the walk's default `halfWidth`, scaled by the hip width), the palm grasp offsets (scaled by the hand), the hanging arm of the stance search (scaled by the arm), and the head landmarks (mouth, head ellipsoid; scaled by the head). The collision body is the X Bot's fitted body refitted to the rig's bone lengths at its height's girth, and the body capsules of the object, floor and contact checks (`qa/geometry.js capsulesFor`) take the same girth. The legs' SELF limits (`thighs overlap` 0.135 m, `shins touch` 0.1 m on the X Bot) scale with the hip width, the finger radius and FINGER_SELF's tolerance with the hand (`landmarks.scale`).
- QA on an adapted rig holds the X Bot's limits, scaled as above. Proportions change timing a little: longer arms move the hands farther a frame, so a reach or a flip that is clean on the X Bot can show a single-frame `POP` on them; retime as its hint says.
- Skills fitted to a scene or a body do not all carry over. The car skills (`exitCar`), `cartwheel` and `catchObject` can report contact issues (`COLLIDE`, `REACH`, `FLOAT`, `SUPPORT`) on other proportions, so read their QA on the rig itself.
- `adaptRig` refuses a stature outside 0.1 to 10 m and names `raw.units`: that is a skeleton in cm declared m, or the reverse.
- `adaptRig` refuses a bone whose `localPos` or `localQuat` holds a non-finite value (NaN, Infinity) or the wrong count, or whose `parent` is not an index into `bones` (-1 for a root) other than its own, and names the bone.
- `adaptRig` refuses a mirrored source (its Left bones on the character's right): the toes point backward from the hips and shoulders. A source without toe bones cannot be checked.
- `registerRig` refuses a shipped rig's id (`x-bot-tpose`, `y-bot-tpose`). Under an id already registered it gives `skeletonFor(id)` a new skeleton and body; a skeleton taken before keeps its own rig's body, whether or not anything asked for it yet.
- Reaches into a scene are not size-similar yet: a table placed at the X Bot's height and distance is a different task for a rig 0.9 or 1.1 its size. Scale the scene and the anchor with the rig (`report.measured.heightM` over the X Bot's 1.82 m) when comparing rigs.

## Left to the compiler (#1078)

Write judgment; the compiler fills what follows from it (`src/motion3/auto-spec.js`, `src/motion3/compiler/auto.js`).

- **Timing:** a key may give `after: "fast" | "normal" | "slow" | <frames>` instead of `f`; the gap comes from how far its channels move. A key may carry `name`, and any frame field (object keys, `use[].f0`, `when`) may say `"@name"` or `"@name+3"`. `frames` left out is `"auto"`.
- **Hips:** `root: [x, z]` leaves the height to the compiler: the standing height, lowered by the reach fixes where the feet or a low target need it.
- **Gaze:** `set.look: "object:<id>" | "anchor:<name>" | [x, y, z] | null` turns the head to the target each frame (an object where it moves); `null` hands the head back to its keys.
- **Legs and free arms:** where no key (yours or a skill's) sets them, knee poles follow the foot's heading, a planted foot keeps its heading and a stepping foot turns to its landing's, and a free arm swings against its own leg while the hips travel. Foot pitch stays flat outside the walk: a heel roll on a keyed ankle path slid the ball. `spec.auto: false` (or `{ knee, foot, arms, look }`) turns them off.
- **Measured:** `motion.compile` output `authored: { keys, values }` counts what you wrote before skills expand; `node tools/agent-time-stats.mjs` breaks a session's time into model, tools and compiles.

## Channels (keys: `[{ f, set: { channel: value }, step?: [channel] }]`)

A channel is interpolated (monotone cubic) only through the keys that set it, so re-key a value to hold it. A `step` key jumps exactly on its frame; use it for visibility switches. Custom channels are hinge angles (`objects`), channels a check names, names ending in `_vis`, and names listed in `spec.channels`.

| Channel | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `root [x,y,z]`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll` | Hips position and body turn |
| `spine_yaw/pitch/roll`, `head_yaw/pitch/roll` | Spread over the chain |
| `foot_l/r [x,y,z]`, `fyaw_l/r`, `fpitch_l/r` | Ankle target, foot heading, toe-down pitch |
| `knee_l/r [x,y,z]` | Body-frame knee direction (default forward) |
| `armw_l/r` | 0 = FK arm (`aswing`, `abend`), 1 = IK to the hand target |
| `hand_l/r [x,y,z]` | World wrist target |
| `w_<anchor>_l/r` | Weight that pulls the hand target to a named anchor (weights add up; the rest is `hand_*`) |
| `elbow_l/r [x,y,z]` | Body-frame elbow direction |
| `wtwist_l/r`, `wflex_l/r` | Wrist twist and flex in degrees |
| `fingers_l/r { relaxed, fist, grip, cigarette, point, spherical, flat, hook }` | Preset weights over relaxed (flat: lying on a face a palm presses, a palm grasp's own; hook: knuckles straight, bent at the middle joints round an edge) |

Flow (`spec.flow`) also leads the head into a turn: `headLead` (default on, 5 frames; a whole number 0..12 sets the frames, `false` turns it off). Walking or standing, the head takes the turn the trunk (`yaw` + `spine_yaw`) makes over the next frames, at most 30 degrees, so the head turns first. It skips any span where your keys move `head_yaw`. `observations.life.lead` reports `[frames the head turns before the pelvis, correlation]`.

## Anchors, props and fits

- Anchors are where a hand goes:
  - `world` — fixed point
  - `body` — relative to the root, turns with the yaw
  - `bone` — offset from a joint
  - `segment` — a point along a bone; with `reach`, it slides until the arm reaches
  - `object` — a point fixed to a scene object, including a hinged door
  - `landmark mouth` — the measured face point
  - `prop` — a point on a prop held by the other hand, plus an optional world `offset` (e.g. 2 cm under the cigarette tip)
- Props are points carried by a hand, given along the palm axes [finger d, knuckle line l, palm normal n].
- Fits move the wrist until an effector (a prop point or fingertips) sits exactly on an anchor while that anchor's weight is on.
- Objects: `objects: { id: { pivot, axis, channel } }` hinges a scene object by a keyed angle channel. Use it for doors.
- Carried objects: `objects: { id: { prop, at, along, visible } }` puts a scene object (a small cylinder, cone or box you placed) on a held prop every frame. `at` is its base along the palm axes [d, l, n] from the prop origin, `along` the palm direction its +Y takes, and `visible` a channel that shows it above 0.5 (step it on when the hand leaves the pocket). Carried objects are not obstacles for QA.
- Spring: `spring: { length, stiffness, damping }` on a carried (keyed included) or free (keys/fall) object makes it swing a beat behind the hand's (or its keys') pushes and settle, e.g. `"bag": { prop: "bag", at: [0.04, 0, 0], along: [1, 0, 0], spring: { length: 0.5, damping: 0.6 } }`.
- `length` (0.05..2 m, default half the object's longest side) is the string or rod length. A natural swing is length 0.4-0.6 m with damping 0.5-0.7.
- `stiffness` (0..500 1/s^2) is how fast it swings back; left out it is 9.81 / length, a real pendulum of that length. `damping` (0..1, default 0.5; 1 settles with no overshoot) is how soon it stops.
- It only turns the object about its base, up to 60 degrees; a still hand leaves it still. The base is the pivot, so put a hanging object's base at the hand with `at`/`along`: a base 0.3 m from the hand swings it from there and every arm swing shakes it harder.
- The swing is computed once at compile time and baked into the take, so scrub, playback and export show the same frames. A hinge refuses it (`spring is not for hinged objects`). On a keyed carry the hand follows the keys and the swing rides on top: REACH measures the hand, not the swing.

## Grasp, weight, throw and catch (`fix.grasp`, on by default)

- Grasp: a carried object with no `at`/`along` (a ball, a can, a cup) rests in the palm where the hand takes it, eases in over 4 frames, and the fingers close until they touch it. Do not pin it on a knuckle point with `at`; pinned grips (a bat, a sword, a pistol) stay as given. `objects.<id>.grasp` names the hold when it matters: `power`, `pinch`, `hook`, `palm`, `spherical` (a ball). QA says when the hand does not hold it: `FINGER_PEN` (a finger in it), `FINGER_GAP` (fewer than three fingertips, or the thumb and two, on it), `PALM_OFF` (a palm or support hold whose palm is off it, the fingertips on it or not), `TIPS_OFF` (a support hold with fewer than three fingertips under the box while it is 0.1 m clear of what is under it), `SUPPORT` (nothing holds it up). `fix: { grasp: false }` keeps the old preset grip; its QA then patches grasp back on. On every frame, with or without a held thing, QA also reads each finger: `FINGER_ROM` (a joint bent back or curled past what a finger does) and `FINGER_SELF` (two fingers lying in each other). Presets keyed together share one curl: weights past a sum of 1 are scaled down. While a hand carries a seated object its grasp preset stays on (the thumb keeps wrapping a ball after reachGrip lets go), and `THUMB_OFF` reports a power or spherical hold whose thumb is off it.
- Weight: `objects.<id>.mass` (kg; a small sphere defaults to a 0.15 kg ball, else its volume as wood) lowers a plain hand target (6 mm per kg, 6 cm at most) and bends the elbow more. `observations.load` lists what each hand carries (kg, most N m). Past 25 N m on one hand QA says `LOAD`; for a right-hand carry its patch adds the left hand on the far face.
- Throw: `throwObject { object, to: [x, y, z], arc: "low" | "high" }` throws what that hand carries: wind-up, swing, release, follow-through. It writes the carry's `release`, a `fall { at, v }` with the launch velocity, and a goal near `to`. The arm sweeps round the shoulder at the release, so QA `FLIP` allows that elbow twice its sweep per frame from 4 frames before a carry's release with a fall `v` to the frame after. A fall with `v` (m/s) leaves with that velocity; `v: "hand"` takes the hand's at the release. A release with no fall stays where the hand let it go.
  `use: [{ skill: "standIdle", f0: 0, args: { at: [0, 0], heading: 0 } }, { skill: "throwObject", f0: 10, args: { object: "ball", to: [0, 0, 5] } }], objects: { ball: { prop: "ballGrip", at: [0, 0, -0.035], along: [0, 1, 0], grab: 4 } }`
- Catch: a flying thing is `objects.<id>.fall { at, v }`. `catchObject { object, side: "r" | "both" }` reaches the first point of the flight in front of the chest the hand can reach, takes it (the carry is filled in), and gives with it 8-15 cm. Out of reach is refused with the nearest frame and point; a hand that comes late is `CATCH "<id> missed by <cm>"`, its patch starts the skill earlier. Over 1 kg it takes both hands.
  `use: [{ skill: "standIdle", f0: 0, args: { at: [0, 0], heading: 0 } }, { skill: "catchObject", f0: 12, args: { object: "ball" } }], objects: { ball: { fall: { at: 12, v: [0, 2.5, -9] } } }` (the ball at [0, 1.6, 6])

## Generators

`{ type: "walk", f0, steps, step, heading: [h0, h1], turnDistance, halfWidth, lift, vary }` plans footsteps from the pose the keys leave at `f0`. Prefer the `walkTo` skill: it expands the same walk at once, so later skills start from where the walk ends.

## QA

Every take gets these checks: body capsules against scene boxes and ground contact, IK reach, planted-foot slide, bone length, speed and pops, knee and elbow flips, knee hyper-extension, hands and props entering the head or the torso, the two hands overlapping, hinged objects passing through other boxes, balance, self contact, a supported fall faster than gravity (`DROP`) and the angular momentum of a flight (`SPIN`).

Balance is style (`observations.balance`, in metres inside the feet) until `checks.limits.balance` (0.08 m) makes it the BALANCE check: the mass-weighted centre of mass (segment fractions) against the support polygon, the convex hull of the heel, ball and tip of the feet on the ground. The subject is `centre of mass outside the feet`, one group however the lean turns; the hint says how far and which way at the worst frame, `centre of mass 0.12 m ahead of the feet (toward +Z)` (ahead, behind, left or right are the body's), and its fix moves `root` toward the polygon's nearest edge, to 1 cm inside the limit.

A take with a walk also gets `observations.gait`, read over the walks only: steps, cadence `[steps/min, "100-115"]`, `stepLength` (m), `speed` (m/s), `stance` `[L, R]` (the share of each foot's stride on the ground), and the pelvis's `rotation` and `obliquity` (deg) and `vertical` and `lateral` (cm), peak to peak per stride. `warnings` lists the values outside a person's walk: cadence outside 80-140, stance outside 0.55-0.65, pelvis rotation outside 3.7-15.3 deg, obliquity outside 2.4-12.4 deg, and vertical outside 2.7-5 cm (provisional). They are warnings, not QA: `qa.ok` does not change. A walk of fewer than 4 steps reports only `steps`. The default `walkTo` is a slow stroll (about 74 steps/min); `period: 14, step: 0.6` walks about 104.

Before a screenshot or an export that has to show why QA said BALANCE or FLOAT, run `motion.physicsOverlay { all: true }` (off: `{ all: false }`): the frame then carries the centre of mass (red past the balance limit, green inside, grey in the air), the support polygon, each air run's hips path (white, dashed) against its gravity parabola (orange) and the spring objects' tip trails.

Declare the task-specific checks in `spec.checks` (skills add their own): `contacts`, `orient`, `below`, `clearance`, `appear`, `supports`, `allow`. Pass the QA numbers to the user as they are. QA proxies are capsules, boxes and an ellipsoid, not the skin.
